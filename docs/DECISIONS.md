# Decision Log

Settled decisions and why. Don't relitigate these without the owner reopening them. Add new entries at the bottom with a date.

| # | Date | Decision | Why | Alternatives considered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026-10-11 | Build from scratch, not decompiled Magic 2014 / Slay the Spire, not a Forge fork. | Clean, maintainable codebase; limited card pool; nice UI; path to selling. Decomp is legally fragile and hard to build on. Forge's pool is too big to balance. | Decomp merge; Forge + roguelike layer; Slay the Spire mod. |
| 2 | 2026-10-11 | Rust rules engine compiled to WASM; TypeScript + React UI. | Rust's strictness catches AI mistakes in the hardest code; owner knows Rust; web UI is ideal for card games and easy to share. | Bevy (weak UI), Unity/C#, Godot, all-TypeScript. |
| 3 | 2026-10-11 | Browser first; Tauri desktop later if commercial. | Easy sharing with friends; same code becomes a small native app. | Native-only executable. |
| 4 | 2026-10-11 | Engine is pure and deterministic (seeded RNG). | Testable, replayable, enables AI search and simulation. | — |
| 5 | 2026-10-11 | Cards are data built from effect primitives. | Scales to hundreds of cards safely; easy swap to original cards later. | Per-card code. |
| 6 | 2026-10-11 | Curated cube of ~250–300 recent cards around 10 two-colour archetypes. | Manageable balance; clear archetypes. | Full Forge pool; single set. |
| 7 | 2026-10-11 | In-duel life (20) and run health (hearts) are separate. | Archetypes like lifegain / pay-life play as intended; variance doesn't end runs. | Life carries over between duels (Slay the Spire style). |
| 8 | 2026-10-11 | Hearts: 3 per act, refill each act; lose 1 per lost duel/elite; 0 = run over; boss loss = run over; remaining hearts buy pre-boss boons. | Losses matter without one bad draw ending a run; each act builds toward the boss. | Fixed per-heart bonus; hearts as boss retries. |
| 9 | 2026-10-11 | **Superseded by #12.** Lands in a separate land pile, one drop per turn. | No mana screw/flood ending runs; no land-ratio busywork. Colour fixing still matters. | Lands in deck + Arena-style hand smoothing (fallback). |
| 10 | 2026-10-11 | Real MTG priority/stack in engine; Arena-style auto-pass in UI. | Keeps Magic's depth without click fatigue. | Simplified no-stack rules. |
| 11 | 2026-10-11 | Real MTG cards for a free prototype only; replace with originals before any sale. | IP. | — |
| 12 | 2026-10-11 | Lands are in the library, as in normal Magic. 7-card opening hand with smoothing (deal several candidate hands, keep the one closest to the deck's land ratio) plus London mulligan. In a run, the game manages basic lands automatically and non-basic lands are cards the player adds. | Every real card works as printed; Magic intuition and card balance carry over; interesting lands become real deck-building choices; no special-case rules. | Land pile with free pick each turn (#9); separate land deck with a choice of which deck to draw from (kept as a future idea: removes screw and flood entirely, but scry, card draw, explore, landfall and mill all change in power or meaning). |
| 13 | 2026-10-11 | Fatigue replaces losing on an empty library: drawing from an empty library costs 1 life, then 2, then 3... | Small decks can run out in a long game; fatigue is a clock, not a cliff. | Normal Magic loss on an empty library. |
| 14 | 2026-10-11 | Opponents follow exactly the same deck, smoothing and mulligan rules as the player. | Symmetry keeps the engine simple and the game fair. | Separate enemy rule (guaranteed 2 lands in hand). |
| 15 | 2026-10-11 | Starting player is a seeded coin flip. | Simple; revisit if it feels bad in a run. | Player always first. |
| 16 | 2026-10-11 | Combat damage follows current Magic rules (no damage assignment order); the engine assigns automatically for now. | The real rule is already the simple one; auto-assignment gets to playable sooner. | Pre-2024 ordering step; manual division from the start. |
| 17 | 2026-10-11 | JSON strings across the wasm boundary; TypeScript types generated with `ts-rs`; duel state held inside wasm. | Debuggable, no hand-mirrored types to drift, no state round-trips. | `serde-wasm-bindgen`; hand-written types. |
| 18 | 2026-10-11 | `sim --games N` plays N games for every pairing of the decks, mirror matches included. Turn cap of 100 turns. It measures land screw (2 or fewer lands at the end of a player's turn 4), flood (60% or more of drawn cards are lands at the end of turn 6) and unspent lands per turn. Thresholds live in `balance.json`. | Steadier win rates; a mirror should be 50/50 between seats, so it shows seat-dependent bugs; screw and flood check that smoothing and the land ratio work. | N games in total; cross pairings only. |
| 19 | 2026-10-11 | Auto-pass stops (`should_auto_pass` in the engine): a player with something to cast is asked when the opponent's spell or ability is on top of the stack, and with an empty stack only in their own main phases, after attackers or blockers are declared, and in the opponent's end step. Their own spell on the stack is left to resolve. The hold-priority toggle asks every time. | With an instant in hand, stopping wherever a spell is castable meant about ten clicks a turn. | Stop whenever any action other than passing is legal. |
| 20 | 2026-10-11 | Cards are played by clicking, with no confirm step and no dragging. A land or a spell without targets is played at once; a spell with targets lights up the legal ones to click. Attackers and blockers are clicked on and off, with `RemoveAttacker` / `RemoveBlocker` as engine actions. | Fastest to build and to play; payment is automatic and final, which the owner accepted. | Confirm step; drag to play. |
| 21 | 2026-10-11 | In a duel against the AI the human is always P1 and the AI P2 (the starting player is still the seeded coin flip). The default matchup is the human on Deck B against Deck A or Deck C, picked from the seed, so a seed alone replays a game. | Fixed seats keep the wasm handle simple; the v1 AI can't pilot Deck B. | Random seats. |
| 22 | 2026-10-11 | One act's map is 7 rows of up to 3 nodes and then the boss: two rows of easy duels, then rows that mix duels with rests and shops, or duels with elites, and a rest before the boss. A row with a choice of kinds never shows only one, and a row with a single node is never an elite. The whole map is visible. Shape and weights are in `balance.json`. | A path is 4 to 5 duels plus the boss, about 20 to 30 minutes; elites are always a choice. | ~15 rows as in Slay the Spire (too long for one act of real Magic duels). |
| 23 | 2026-10-11 | A drawn duel costs no heart and pays a little gold; the node is cleared. A drawn boss duel is fought again with the boons kept. Conceding a duel is allowed and counts as a loss. | A draw is nobody's fault; the boss has to be beaten. Conceding saves time in a hopeless game. | A draw counts as a loss. |
| 24 | 2026-10-11 | A rest site offers one of: restore a heart, or remove a card. It can be left without doing either. Upgrades wait for M4. | Both are real choices already; healing keeps a bad start from ending the run. | Removal only. |
| 25 | 2026-10-11 | The two planeswalkers are the Warden (green-white, "whenever a creature enters under your control, you gain 1 life") and the Firebrand (black-red, "creatures you control have haste"). Each starts with 15 commons; lands are added automatically. | The AI can pilot both, so the sim's fairness numbers can be trusted. | Blue-black control (the sim would under-rate it). |
| 26 | 2026-10-11 | Relics, boons, planeswalker passives and boss emblems are all `ModifierDef`s. A modifier changes starting life, the opening hand, adds a starting basic land, gives its owner an emblem built from card abilities, or (relics only) pays gold per win. Emblems support upkeep and creature-enters triggers, anthems and keyword grants. Tokens, scry, cost reduction and a free mulligan were left out of M3. | One route into the duel through `PlayerConfig`; nothing needs a rule outside the current scope. | Per-relic code. |
| 27 | 2026-10-11 | Boons (one per remaining heart, each once, for the boss duel only): +5 life, +1 opening card, boss at -5 life, a basic land on the battlefield. The boss starts at 25 life and makes the player lose 1 life in each of its upkeeps. Elites are stronger decks without emblems. | Modest edges, as GAME_DESIGN asks; the boss's clock makes the boons matter. | Token-making boss (needs tokens). |
| 28 | 2026-10-11 | Automatic basic lands: lands are 40% of the finished deck, split by the coloured mana symbols of the spells (largest remainder), with at least 2 lands for any colour the deck uses. A non-basic land replaces a basic of one of its colours. The player's deck is stored without basics; they are worked out each time. Enemy decks are hand-built with their lands. | No land busywork; Deck B's spells come out at 6 Swamp and 4 Island, which suits its double-black costs. | Counting cards per colour; a fixed 50/50 split. |
| 29 | 2026-10-11 | A card reward is three different cards: two from the planeswalker's colours and one from any colour, picked by rarity weights that shift up for elites; 15% of the time the last is a dual land instead. Skipping is allowed. Shops sell three cards, one dual land and one relic, and remove one card. | With a small pool, colour-weighting keeps rewards usable while leaving a splash possible. | Rewards from own colours only. |
| 30 | 2026-10-11 | Gold: 30 starting; 30 for a duel win, 50 for an elite, 10 for a loss or a draw (nothing for a lost elite); +10 for winning at 15 or more life and +10 for winning within 14 turns (both players' turns counted). Prices: common 40, uncommon 70, rare 110, land 50, relic 120, removal 60. | Starting numbers for playtesting; all in `balance.json`. | — |
| 31 | 2026-10-11 | The attacking player divides combat damage by hand only when an attacker has two or more blockers. The automatic division is filled in and can be confirmed with one click; each change moves one point. The AI keeps the automatic division. | It only asks where there is a real choice. | Asking for every blocked attacker; a drag-to-divide control. |
| 32 | 2026-10-11 | The duel of a run lives inside `RunState`, set up by the run. The run offers `Concede` while it is being played and `FinishDuel` once it has a result; nothing about the duel's outcome is decided outside the engine. | The run stays one serialisable state, so the same seed and actions replay it, duels included, and saving (M5) is just serialising it. | A separate duel handle that TypeScript reports the result of. |
| 33 | 2026-10-11 | Run content (planeswalkers, enemies, relics) is data in `data/run/`, with its numbers and texts side by side like a card's. Boons and every other run number are in `data/balance.json`. | Content reads like cards; tuning numbers stay in one file. | Everything in `balance.json`. |
| 34 | 2026-10-11 | The engine can say what its AI would do in the human's place (`duel_suggest` on the run handle). With `?hints` in the address the page exposes it, and the end-to-end tests follow it by clicking the real screen. It is not shown to the player. | Lets a browser test play well enough to reach and beat the boss, while still clicking only what a player could. | A test-only cheat that sets the duel's result. |
